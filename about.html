About us

Built for the awkward middle.

Between “I have this idea” and actually doing it there's a stretch of nothing — sometimes months, sometimes years. MotivateYoungMinds exists because nobody was building anything for that stretch.

Everyone helps kids refine ideas. Almost nobody helps them begin.

Look around and you'll find plenty of programs for young people with ambition: pitch competitions, incubators, plan-writing workshops. All of them assume you've already started. For the kid whose idea has lived in a notebook for two years, they're useless.

The actual blockers are quieter. Fear of looking stupid. Not knowing what a first step even looks like. Having nobody notice when you don't follow through. None of those get solved by a workshop. They get solved by a person who checks in on Friday.

That's the whole insight here. Less a program, more a promise: someone will notice, and someone will care whether you start.

Our lane

We hold the flashlight. You walk.

In scope

  • Motivational coaching and genuine encouragement
  • Advice on getting unstuck and building momentum
  • Accountability — check-ins, goal-setting, gentle nagging
  • A peer community so you're not starting alone
  • Mentorship on mindset, habits, and follow-through

Out of scope

  • Developing or executing your idea for you
  • Rating, ranking, or validating ideas
  • Business planning, pitch decks, consulting
  • Guilt-tripping anyone about productivity
  • Pretending starting isn't scary. It is. Just not alone.

That boundary isn't a limitation — it's the point. If we built it for you, it would be ours. When it finally ships, you'll know every bit of it was you. We just made sure you didn't quit in month two.

The honest version

No founding myth. Just these three things.

Ask anyone who's ever started something real about their beginning. These three objects always show up — including for us.

Wk 0 · The notebook

…maybe someday

One: a notebook nobody opened.

Full of ideas that have been “almost ready” for months. Every starter we meet has one of these — and it's not a character flaw, it's just what an unstarted thing looks like.

you still gonna start that podcast or what

idk. eventually. it's complicated

friday. send ONE email about it. that's the whole assignment

ok fine. friday.

Two: week one sounds like this chat.

Assignments that are deliberately, almost embarrassingly small — because an email sent on Friday beats a plan perfected for a year. Someone who notices is the whole trick.

> wk_01 :: sent first email [done]

> wk_02 :: show plan, one page [done]

> wk_03 :: rough intro recorded [done]

> wk_06 :: episode 01 live

Three: by week six, the log does the talking.

Not a business plan — a streak. That's the whole thesis of this organization: momentum you can point to beats motivation you merely feel.

Who's behind this? Volunteers who were each once the person with the notebook. Same laptops, same half-finished drafts — just a few years further along, and now the ones checking in on Friday.

What we believe

Three things we tell every starter

01

Feeling stuck is normal

Every person you admire once sat exactly where you are, staring at a blank page. Stuck is a place, not a personality.

02

Small beats perfect

You don't need the master plan today. You need one step small enough that you can't talk yourself out of it. Then another tomorrow.

03

The win has to be yours

Which is why we'll never do it for you. We'd rather watch you take three messy months than hand you a finished thing that isn't really yours.
