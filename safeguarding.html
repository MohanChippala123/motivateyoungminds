Safeguarding

Safety isn't a feature. It's the frame.

Before any mentor talks to any teen, these are the rules the program will be built on. Written down before it launches, not after something goes wrong.

What the program will require — once it exists

If you're a parent or school reading this before the program formally launches: these are the rules we're putting in writing before the first mentor talks to the first teen. Nothing below is live yet.

Vetting before anyone talks

Any mentor matched with a minor will pass a background check and complete youth-protection training before the first call. References will be checked. This is not optional.

Communication on observable channels

All check-ins will happen over program-run video or a moderated group chat — no private DMs, no personal social accounts. Parents can ask to review what their teen's been talking about.

Consent before matching

Anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian to approve before being matched with a mentor. Anyone under 13 doesn't get a 1-on-1 match at all — only group resources.

Reporting that works

Report anything uncomfortable at mohan0512vittal@gmail.com with the subject "safety." Before launch, every report will be read and answered by the program founder directly. Nothing goes unread.

Consequences that actually apply

Any rule broken: suspension while it's investigated, removal if it's real. Mandatory reporting obligations apply to everything we know about. We'd rather lose a mentor than have "we'll look into it" as the answer.
